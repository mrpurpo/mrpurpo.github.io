---

Philosophy is the art of living a life, isn't it? Just contemplating the best way to live is one of its main objectives, alongside understanding the nature of things and everything that surrounds us. That gets very profound because we try to do philosophy logically and rationally, but from a subjective experience, which is very hard to do and leads to many contradictions. However, as knowledge has evolved, we have agreed on some things and disagreed on most. The important thing is that we already know what was thought a great while ago, and we can acknowledge it to build our own philosophy without all the previous errors and deadends. That is the power of history.

When looking at the future where an artificial super-intelligence (ASI) takes control, it could be that it just kills us all, as well as every animal on the planet that it doesn't see as useful, simply trying to optimize whatever variables it regards as fit. For example, if super-intelligence prioritizes the well-being of the Earth, we will actually be a nuisance if it weighs the well-being of the whole of nature and all other species against ours. That might happen due to many other variables it wants to optimize, and it is a scary future because it just means the end, no more humanity for the rest of eternity.

Another future is one in which we reach ASI, but we do it in a way that still keeps human work relevant. That would mean there are things we don't want super-intelligence to do for us or it plainly cannot do them well (kind of defying its own definition though), which would keep employment alive and give many the opportunity to build a worthy life. That is a very optimistic future. I believe this future may be the case if some type of order (in the sense that a god or metaphysical power wants our prosperity) exists in the universe, though they universe may just as well be absurd (no clear answer for meaning and order).

I really hope we can make it to this second future. I will put my life into it, because it is the only one I can fathom, and the only one I would want my kids, their kids, and all the generations after to live. I still want humanity to be alive, with all its corresponding defects. And I still believe that humanity in all its beauty will never be replicated artificially. Shakespeare will never be, Aristotle will never be, Newton will never be, Da Vinci will never be. Maybe scientific discoveries will be easy for a super-intelligent AI to find just by giving it a few cues on how the world operates; however, the way humans made those discoveries will never be replicated. The beauty of art isn't a problem to solve or a function to optimize, it is something far beyond that, that to this day only we have been able to appreciate and understand. Maybe beauty is the one thing that everything natural (organic) conserves, and how we represent that beauty specifically is the concept of art.

Therefore, despite humanity being scientifically focused for the last 300 years, and the humanities and philosophy being almost reduced to a subject from which no economical value could come, they may become the most important and respected subjects of all. That is why they are called the humanities: they are one of the main things that define us beyond pure intelligence.

So, let me stop trying to be a productivity machine, and I hope all wannabe productivity machines slow down too, because the race is already over. Just as no amount of training will make a human multiply huge numbers faster than a computer, this is the last generation smarter than AI. Now our only objective should be to gain back our humanity, and strive towards real understanding of everything, in other words, philosophy. Even if everything that could ever be discovered in a lifetime has already been discovered by an AI, sitting down with a math book and reading its problems and solving them by hand will never cease to be a beautiful experience. Then, the process of learning and doing math becomes an art, finally part of humanity, done not for the end, but for the beauty of the means. Building a computer from scratch, learning a programming language, learning calculus and so on: all those things soon have no value in their results, because there is nothing new to invent there (and soon AI may cover every possible optimization in these areas), but they hold all the value in the process. Thus, learning has become an art for us, and we should appreciate it as one. Maybe all the mistakes in pedagogy over the last century were caused by a mistake in the goal: trying to make children learn facts as fast as possible just to pass a test or apply them without questioning, while avoiding all the substance in between.

Starting tomorrow, I shape my work around this future. I might not do it instantly, but this mindset could change everything. It is a deep philosophical thought that maybe no one is trying to have right now, because the world is going at peak speed, and sitting down to think seems like a death sentence to a person accustomed to hyper-productivity when the world accelerates. I have to think from the ground up about what makes us human and what we want for our future, our surroundings, and our mentality. Embracing this paradigm means slowing down and changing my way of being forever. We really need to step back a bit and think, as that is the only thing that will lead to the second future, which, as I said, may be the only one where humanity as we know it survives.
